**The big picture:** both plans clearly worked from the real paper — Figure 4 does decompose ESMC's latent space into biochemical, structural, and functional principles across levels of biological complexity via sparse autoencoders trained on layer-60 representations of ESMC 6B, and both correctly reconstruct all seven panels (A–G) at a structural level. Where they diverge is in numeric precision and, in one case, what a panel actually *contains*.

**On the Panel F error specifically:** this matters more than a stray number, because it's a claim about what the panel *is*, not a supporting statistic. If gptluna's notebook is built as specified, Panel F would show the wrong content entirely — a plan can't be called "faithful" for a panel where it's reconstructing the wrong dataset as the headline visual, however well-labeled the supplementary S37 extension is.

**On gptluna's numeric rigor**, though, credit where due — the SAE provenance details in its Phase 1 (TopK sweep of 8/16/32/64/128, codebook sweep 2¹³–2¹⁷, 8 billion SAE training tokens, d_model=2560, primary analysis at layer 60 with TopK=64) all check out exactly. alphaxiv never states most of these. One gptluna number I flag as unverified rather than wrong: the UniRef90/MGnify/JGI training-split percentages (35.7/10.7/53.6%) don't reconcile with the raw sequence counts stated in the paper's LM training section — though token-weighted mixture ratios often do diverge from raw corpus proportions, so this could still be correct if it reflects actual training weights rather than corpus size. Similarly, the "531 kinase-universal + 254 family-specific" split and the "119 enzymes / 17-of-21" Panel C figures aren't confirmable from the visible text — plausible appendix content, but I can't verify them.

**Interactivity is a genuine split, not a tie.** alphaxiv is written as an actual notebook: concrete `mo.ui` widget types, explicit hover/click/brush/toggle behavior per panel, and real cross-panel reactive linking (click a feature in A → drives both D and B; click a row in F → drives G). It reads like something you could start building directly. gptluna is written as an engineering roadmap — phases, a shared data layer, a verification checklist — and its "Interactive Controls" section is a reasonable enumeration but never gets tied to specific widgets or cross-cell reactivity the way alphaxiv's does. What gptluna offers instead is architecturally more ambitious: a second, GPU/HuggingFace-backed notebook that recomputes Panels C and E live from arbitrary input sequences rather than only browsing precomputed artifacts — a materially higher interactivity ceiling, just not spelled out at the UI level.

**Verdict:** for pure per-panel numeric fidelity, gptluna is the stronger document — its category counts, feature IDs, and SAE provenance read like they were pulled straight from the paper's methods section. But alphaxiv is the more faithful *and* more interactive plan overall, because it gets the one structural point gptluna gets wrong (what Panel F actually is) and pairs whole-figure correctness with concretely buildable interaction design rather than an interaction-controls checklist. If you're actually building this, the efficient move is synthesis: take gptluna's data manifest, category counts, and technical provenance rigor, but use alphaxiv's Panel F framing (81-proteome heatmap as the real panel, not a supplement) and its cell-level widget/reactive-linking design as the notebook skeleton.
